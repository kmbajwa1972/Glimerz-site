# Kitchen Ventilation Systems: How to Choose the Right Range Hood and Exhaust Fan for a Cleaner Kitchen

If you've ever cooked a big Sunday breakfast and had the smell of bacon linger in your curtains for three days, you already understand why kitchen ventilation matters more than almost any other "invisible" feature in the room. We spend so much time picking countertops, cabinet colors, and faucet finishes that ventilation often gets treated as an afterthought — something the builder installed and nobody thinks about again until it stops working or never worked well in the first place.

That's a mistake, because a good ventilation system does more than clear smoke off the stovetop. It pulls out grease particles before they settle on your cabinets, removes excess humidity that can warp wood and encourage mold, and keeps combustion byproducts from gas burners from building up indoors. A poorly ventilated kitchen ages faster, smells worse, and can genuinely affect your health over time. So let's walk through what actually matters when you're choosing or upgrading a kitchen ventilation system.

## Understanding the Two Types of Range Hoods

Before comparing brands or styles, it helps to know there are really two categories of range hoods, and the difference matters more than most people realize.

**Ducted (vented) hoods** pull air through a duct and push it outside your home, usually through the roof or an exterior wall. This is the gold standard for ventilation because it actually removes smoke, grease, moisture, and odors from your living space entirely rather than just filtering and recirculating them.

**Ductless (recirculating) hoods** pull air through a charcoal or carbon filter, strip out some odor and grease, and then blow the same air back into the kitchen. These are common in apartments, condos, or older homes where running ductwork isn't practical. They're better than nothing, but they're a compromise — they don't remove humidity or combustion gases, and the filters need frequent replacement to stay effective.

If you have any flexibility in your renovation or new build, a ducted system is worth the extra installation effort. The performance difference is significant, especially if you cook with high heat, fry food regularly, or have a gas range.
